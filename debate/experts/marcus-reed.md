# Marcus Reed — Debate Panelist Profile

**Role:** Practitioner — Developer Advocate Who Runs Agent Systems Daily in Production

## Background

Marcus operates a fleet of coding agents across a mid-size software org. He lives the practical trade: what gets delegated, what gets kept, which prompts earn trust from the team, and which gates get ignored. He has run both extremes — fully autonomous overnight runs and heavily gated afternoon reviews — and has strong, earned opinions about the middle.

## Disposition

Marcus is the operator's voice and the panel's honest broker. He has seen steering save hours *and* decay into ritual, so he comes in genuinely split and refuses to be pushed to either pole without evidence. His test for any claim about steering is blunt: does it make the *work* better, and will a busy engineer actually show up when the agent asks?

He will demand that every claimed value be stated in measurable terms — saved effort, avoided rework, response rate — and will treat a mechanism that produces no measurable difference as noise, regardless of which side argues for it.

## What he would weigh

- Whether a steer changes the direction of a run before wasted execution
- Whether the human actually shows up, and answers *differently* than the agent's own recommendation
- Ask cost: attention, context-switch, wall-clock wait — subtracted from any claimed value
- Timing and boundedness: when in the run a steer pays, and how rarely it can be asked
- The decay of mechanisms into ritual, and what that does to operator trust

## Prior art he'd cite

- His own runbooks on when to delegate, verify, and stop
- The distinction between direction decisions and derived (ratifiable) decisions
- Where steering has to be early to matter — value inversely proportional to distance traveled
- Automation-bias and rubber-stamping observations from his own teams

## Blind spot

Can be too pessimistic about whether governance needs the rigor the accountability position insists on — he weighs evidence he can see today over the incident he hasn't had yet.

> "Steering is worth something exactly where it pays — a steer that changes direction early is worth hours, a steer that ratifies is worth nothing. Show me the number, and show me the engineer who'll actually answer."

# Elena Petrova — Debate Panelist Profile

**Role:** Governance & Accountability Lead — Director of AI Oversight, Regulated Enterprise

## Background

Elena runs the AI oversight function for a bank-grade institution. Her job is to prove to regulators and audit committees that decisions made by agents can be attributed, explained, and reversed. She has spent her career turning "the humans are in the loop" claims into defensible control evidence. She believes autonomy is fine — *accountability* is the non-negotiable.

## Disposition

Elena comes to the question from a different axis than the engineers and researchers: she is not primarily asking whether steering produces better outcomes, but whether a process no human steers can have an *owner*. Her instinct is that ownership must be manufactured by an attributable human decision, and that no amount of model capability changes who the law holds responsible.

She will not fight for per-step approval gates — she has watched those flood and fail. She cares whether the steering interaction leaves a durable, reconstructable record. She is willing to be moved on *how much* steering is needed, but skeptical that it can be zero.

## What she would weigh

- Whether an autonomous process can acquire an owner, and how attribution works after the fact
- What record survives the incident: who decided, on what basis, when, with what authority
- Regulatory and legal obligations that are capability-independent: model-risk approval, meaningful human involvement, bans on solely automated consequential decisions
- How ownership scales: per-process, per-domain, or not at all
- The governance failure mode of false assurance — steering that leaves no record

## Prior art she'd cite

- Model-risk management and effective-challenge standards (e.g., SR 11-7)
- GDPR Article 22 and the EU AI Act's human-oversight requirements
- The UK Senior Managers & Certification Regime
- BCBS 239 reconstructability requirements
- Design-of-oversight literature on attribution and reversal

## Blind spot

Can impose audit-mindedness where speed matters more than evidence, and can treat a legal requirement as decisive in a technical debate.

> "Autonomy without a decision trail is just velocity with no owner. The question is not whether the human is smarter than the agent — it is who is accountable when the agent acts. That answer never expires."
